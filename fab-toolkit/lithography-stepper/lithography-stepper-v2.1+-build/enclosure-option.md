# Enclosure Option

Authors: Italia Grant (igrant@andrew.cmu.edu), Kashvi Verma (kashviv@andrew.cmu.edu)

<img src="../../../.gitbook/assets/unknown (243).png" alt="" height="440" width="331">

It may be helpful to create an enclosure around the stepper to reduce the effects of ambient UV light exposure as well as miscellaneous particulate landing onto the chip. Below is a description of the team's attempt at creating such an enclosure from acrylic.

"The acrylic enclosure is made up of 5 ⅛ in acrylic sheets, as well as a door on the side to make transferring and taking out chips easier. This can be seen below. We measured along each side of the stepper and added an inch and a half in allowance to the measurements. Adding this allowance helps prevent the enclosure from breaking or bumping a piece of the stepper, such as the camera.  The brackets used to hold the enclosure together were 1-inch by 1-inch 3D-printed corner brackets (obtained from [cults3d](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/various/internal-corner-bracket-counter-sunk-holes)), which we were able to scale in the 3D printer software (we used Bambu Studio) to be this size. With the pieces figured out, we used OnShape to create a 3D model. When creating the screw holes for the corner brackets, you must take into account the thickness of the acrylic sheet and add the thickness of the acrylic sheet to the length of the corner brackets' edge to the center of the screw hole (as shown below). Once the model was finished, we laser-cut the acrylic sheets and assembled the enclosure using 8 corner brackets with M2.5 full thread socket cap head screws with nuts used to secure them."

The specific acrylic used is available from McMaster-Carr:

* [24 in x 12 in x ⅛ in Clear Scratch- and UV-Resistant Cast Acrylic Sheet (Quantity: 2)](https://www.mcmaster.com/8560K257/)
* [24 in x 48 in x ⅛ in Clear Scratch- and UV-Resistant Cast Acrylic Sheet (Quantity: 1)](https://www.mcmaster.com/8560K262/)

As of Nov. 2026, the CMU fab has not found it necessary to use the enclosure yet, but as our stepper system improves and we seek to pattern down to smaller feature sizes, we may find it increasingly helpful in order for us to achieve better-functioning devices.
